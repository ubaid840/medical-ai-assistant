
# Global Pharmacopeia & Essential Medicines Compendium

This master database contains pharmacological data, mechanisms of action, and clinical indications for the major classes of medical therapeutics used worldwide.

## 1. Antimicrobials & Antibiotics
* **Beta-Lactams (Penicillins & Cephalosporins):** 
  * *Mechanism:* Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs).
  * *Key Drugs:* Amoxicillin, Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Zosyn), Ceftriaxone, Cefepime.
  * *Indications:* Strep throat, pneumonia, syphilis, severe sepsis.
* **Macrolides:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Inhibit bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit.
  * *Key Drugs:* Azithromycin, Clarithromycin.
* **Fluoroquinolones:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Inhibit bacterial DNA topoisomerase IV and DNA gyrase.
  * *Key Drugs:* Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin. (Warning: Risk of tendon rupture).

## 2. Cardiovascular & Antihypertensives
* **ACE Inhibitors:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Prevent conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, leading to vasodilation.
  * *Key Drugs:* Lisinopril, Enalapril. (Side effect: dry cough).
* **Beta-Blockers:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Block sympathetic stimulation at beta-1 adrenergic receptors in the heart, reducing heart rate and contractility.
  * *Key Drugs:* Metoprolol, Carvedilol, Propranolol.
* **Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs):** 
  * *Mechanism:* Block calcium influx into smooth muscle cells, causing vasodilation.
  * *Key Drugs:* Amlodipine (Dihydropyridine), Diltiazem (Non-dihydropyridine).

## 3. Endocrinology & Antidiabetics
* **Biguanides:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Decrease hepatic glucose production and increase insulin sensitivity.
  * *Key Drug:* Metformin. (First-line for Type 2 Diabetes).
* **GLP-1 Receptor Agonists:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Stimulate insulin release in a glucose-dependent manner, delay gastric emptying, and suppress appetite.
  * *Key Drugs:* Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), Liraglutide, Dulaglutide.
* **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Inhibit glucose reabsorption in the proximal renal tubules, causing glycosuria.
  * *Key Drugs:* Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin. (Also used for heart failure).

## 4. Oncology & Antineoplastics
* **Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs):** 
  * *Mechanism:* Block specific enzymes (kinases) involved in cancer cell growth.
  * *Key Drugs:* Imatinib (BCR-ABL in CML), Osimertinib (EGFR in Lung Cancer).
* **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Block PD-1 or CTLA-4 proteins, allowing the patient's T-cells to recognize and attack cancer cells.
  * *Key Drugs:* Pembrolizumab (Keytruda), Nivolumab (Opdivo), Ipilimumab.

## 5. Neuro-Psychiatry
* **Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs):** 
  * *Mechanism:* Inhibit the reuptake of serotonin in the synaptic cleft, increasing mood and emotional regulation.
  * *Key Drugs:* Sertraline (Zoloft), Fluoxetine (Prozac), Escitalopram (Lexapro).
* **Atypical Antipsychotics:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Antagonize dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors.
  * *Key Drugs:* Quetiapine, Olanzapine, Aripiprazole, Risperidone.

## 6. Hematology & Anticoagulants
* **Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs):** 
  * *Mechanism:* Directly inhibit specific factors in the coagulation cascade (usually Factor Xa or Thrombin).
  * *Key Drugs:* Apixaban (Eliquis), Rivaroxaban (Xarelto), Dabigatran.
* **Vitamin K Antagonists:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase, preventing synthesis of factors II, VII, IX, X.
  * *Key Drug:* Warfarin (Requires regular INR monitoring).

## 7. Analgesics & Anti-Inflammatories
* **Nonsteroidal Anti-inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs):** 
  * *Mechanism:* Reversibly inhibit COX-1 and COX-2 enzymes, decreasing prostaglandin synthesis.
  * *Key Drugs:* Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Celecoxib (COX-2 selective).
* **Opioids:** 
  * *Mechanism:* Agonists at the mu-opioid receptors in the central nervous system.
  * *Key Drugs:* Morphine, Fentanyl, Oxycodone. (High risk of respiratory depression and addiction).
